Personal project · Embedded systems

IoT Laundry Status Monitor

A washing machine can pause or shake lightly without being done. I built an external monitor that reads the machine’s vibration and tells me when the cycle actually ends.

  1. Vibration measurement MPU-6500 accelerometer
  2. Cycle-end detection ESP32 firmware in C++
  3. Status display and alerts TFT display and webhook notifications
How I built it
I used an MPU-6500 accelerometer to capture vibration. An ESP-WROOM-32 runs my C++ detection logic, reports status and elapsed time to an onboard TFT, and sends a webhook push notification to my phone at the end of the cycle.
What I tested
I adjusted the vibration threshold and time window over 20 test cycles so small background vibrations wouldn’t trigger a false alert. The tuned setup achieved 95% cycle-detection accuracy in those tests.
  • C++
  • ESP32
  • MPU-6500
  • TFT
  • 3D printing

Team project · University of Waterloo Electric Vehicle Challenge

Getting a competition EV to the track

My work spanned battery selection, electrical troubleshooting, mechanical assembly, and testing.

Battery trade-off
I compared conventional lithium-ion with lower-cost lithium iron phosphate cells. I weighed capacity, weight, and compatibility with the vehicle and selected the lithium-ion pack.
System checks
I designed a multistep validation plan and carried out 25 tests across software, electrical, and other vehicle systems. I found loose wiring before the vehicle ran and repaired it by soldering.
Track result
I helped assemble the vehicle, documented design decisions and test results, and incorporated feedback from industry mentors. Our team completed every competition track run.
